    How it was made

    Picture
    Procedural 3D in three.js r186: HDR, depth of field, bloom and a film grade
    Score
    Orchestral trailer score synthesised offline with Web Audio at 120 BPM
    Voice
    British narrator, Kokoro text-to-speech (bm_george)
    Type
    Cinzel, Cormorant Garamond, IBM Plex Mono
    Render
    Headless Chromium, 30 fps, open-matte from a 2.39 master

    Watch in VR & AR

    The real-time film also runs in a VR headset and as an AR model on your table (WebXR). Open the film's own address on the device: https://aiprdesign.github.io/wc/. Embedded previews such as the claude.ai page block VR and AR. iPhone and iPad: Safari has no WebXR yet, so use the normal film or Experience mode there.

    1. VR · what you need

      A headset with a WebXR browser: Meta Quest 2 / 3 / 3S / Pro (Meta Quest Browser), Pico 4, Apple Vision Pro (Safari, visionOS 2), or a PC headset with Chrome or Edge.

    2. VR · start it

      Put the headset on, open its browser and go to the address above. When the film has loaded, choose Watch in VR and allow the immersive view. For the slow-motion flythrough without narration, start Experience mode first, then press VR in the player bar.

    3. VR · controls

      Trigger or A / X plays and pauses, the thumbstick left / right skips 5 seconds, grip leaves VR, and on Vision Pro a pinch plays and pauses. Sit or stand still: the camera glides for you, cuts fade through black, and chapter headings float ahead of you.

    4. AR · what you need

      An Android phone with Chrome and Google Play Services for AR (ARCore), or a Meta Quest 3 / 3S with passthrough.

    5. AR · place the film

      Open the address above in Chrome, turn the sound on and choose View in AR, then allow the camera. Point at a table and move the phone slowly until a gold ring sits on the surface, then tap to place the film: each shot appears as a small model in a glass case. Tap again to move it, and walk around it to see it from any side. Use the on-screen play / pause and Exit AR buttons. On Quest 3: trigger places, A / X plays and pauses, grip leaves AR.

    Install it as an app (Android): open the film in Chrome, tap Install as an app on the start screen (or Chrome's menu → Install app / Add to Home screen). It gets its own icon, opens full screen, and runs on Chrome's engine, so View in AR works inside it. Long-press the icon for shortcuts straight to AR, VR or Experience mode. (A plain Android WebView app can't do AR; to publish it on Google Play, wrap this installable app as a Trusted Web Activity, e.g. with pwabuilder.com.)

    Tip: AR finds surfaces best on a plain, well-lit table with some texture (wood, cloth), not glass or a glossy white top. If a button says it needs a headset or an AR phone, this device or browser can't open that mode; tapping it shows these steps.

    Run the code

    The film is a website that draws itself in real time. It has no installer and no build step: you only need to serve the folder from a small local web server and open it in a browser.

    1. Download and unzip the source

      Press Download source (.zip) in the Source code section below and unzip it. You get a folder called achievements-of-western-civilization.

    2. Open a terminal in that folder

      On Windows, open the folder, click the address bar, type cmd and press Enter. On macOS, right-click the folder and choose New Terminal at Folder. Or type:

      cd achievements-of-western-civilization

    3. Start a local web server

      Use whichever you already have. With Node.js:

      npx http-server -p 8080
      or Python 3 
      python3 -m http.server 8080

      Leave this window open while you watch. Opening index.html by double-clicking does not work, because browsers block the film's modules from local files.

    4. Open the film in your browser

      http://localhost:8080

      Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Wait a few seconds while the score is composed, then press Play film (sound starts when you press Play).

    Add to the addressWhat it does
    #squareSquare 1:1 frame
    #16x9Landscape 16:9 frame
    #9x16Vertical 9:16 frame for phones (also ?aspect=9:16)
    #2x3Vertical 2:3 frame (also ?aspect=2:3)
    #4x5Vertical 4:5 frame for LinkedIn and social feeds (1080 × 1350) (also ?aspect=4:5)
    #experienceOpens Experience mode (combine with a format: #2x3&experience)
    #wide or nothingThe original 2.39 widescreen frame
    ?q=high or ?q=ultraSharper, more lifelike picture on a strong graphics card: ambient occlusion, finer shadows and surface detail, lens glare and finer bokeh (use ?q=low on slower computers)
    ?novoPlays without the narrator
    ?noaudioSilent, and loads faster

    Combine options with &, for example http://localhost:8080/?q=high#9x16. In the player, Space plays and pauses, F goes full screen, M mutes, R records a video live (MP4 in Chrome, Edge and Safari; a slow device can drop frames), and the buttons save the soundtrack as WAV and switch the frame format.

    Export video (frame-perfect): press the download-arrow button in the player bar (or Download as video on the start screen, or X). The film is rendered frame by frame, not in real time, so every frame is kept however slow your device is. Pick 720p, 1080p or screen size. High quality adds motion blur and supersampling, which is slower. You get an MP4 with the soundtrack (WebM in browsers without an H.264 encoder) in the frame format you are watching. The soundtrack is included even when the player is muted (the score without the narrator with ?novo). The file is always the film itself: Experience mode, Explore and the live camera step aside while it renders and come back afterwards. A progress bar shows the frames done and the time left; Cancel stops it without a download. Keep the tab open (it carries on in the background). It needs a current Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. Otherwise, use npm run render:16x9 below.

    Look around while it plays: the film is interactive. Drag to orbit the camera, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to recentre; it glides back to the director's view a few seconds after you let go.

    Experience mode: a slow-motion flythrough with no narration. Press Experience mode on the start screen (or open the film with #experience, e.g. #square&experience). A drone camera glides around every shot at 0.35× speed (the speed button cycles 0.35×, 0.5×, 1× and 0.25×) to an ambient score made live in the browser, and it loops. Drag to steer, scroll or pinch to zoom, press Esc to return to the film.

    VR and AR: press Watch in VR or View in AR on the start screen. See Watch in VR & AR above for devices, steps and controls.

    Explore in 3D: pause anywhere and press E (or the Explore 3D button) to step into the frozen scene. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag to pan, fly with W A S D and R / F, double-click to reset, and press E or Esc to return.

    Render the MP4 videos yourself

    Your own computer renders the videos much faster than a cloud machine, because it uses your graphics card. You need Node.js (version 18 or newer). Everything else installs with one command.

    1. Install the tools, once

      In the project folder, run this. It downloads the local server, a copy of Chromium to render with, and ffmpeg to make the MP4.

      npm install

    2. Render the format you want

      npm run render:1x1
      npm run render:16x9
      npm run render:9x16
      npm run render:2x3
      or all four in a row 
      npm run render:all

      Browser windows open and close by themselves while it renders: that is how it reaches your graphics card, so leave them alone. The terminal shows progress and the time left.

    3. Find your videos

      Each MP4 lands in the renders folder, with the music and narration included:

      CommandVideoSize
      render:1x1renders/1x1/achievements-of-western-civilization-1x1.mp41080 × 1080
      render:16x9renders/16x9/achievements-of-western-civilization-16x9.mp41920 × 1080
      render:9x16renders/9x16/achievements-of-western-civilization-9x16.mp41080 × 1920

    If a render stops part-way, run the same command again: finished frames are kept and it carries on where it left off. Advanced options (frame rate, a time range, more workers) are listed at the top of tools/render.mjs.
